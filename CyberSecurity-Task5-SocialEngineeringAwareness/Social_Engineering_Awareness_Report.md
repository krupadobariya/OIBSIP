# Social Engineering Awareness Report

## Introduction

Social engineering is a technique used by attackers to manipulate people into revealing confidential information or performing actions that compromise security. Instead of attacking computers directly, attackers exploit human behavior.

---

## Types of Social Engineering Attacks

### 1. Phishing
Phishing is an attack where fake emails, messages, or websites are used to trick users into sharing passwords, bank details, or personal information.

**Prevention**
- Verify the sender before clicking links.
- Avoid opening suspicious attachments.
- Enable two-factor authentication (2FA).

---

### 2. Vishing
Vishing (Voice Phishing) is a phone call scam where attackers pretend to be bank officials, company employees, or government officers to obtain sensitive information.

**Prevention**
- Never share OTPs or passwords over the phone.
- Verify the caller's identity.
- Hang up if the call seems suspicious.

---

### 3. Smishing
Smishing is phishing through SMS messages. Attackers send fake messages containing malicious links or requests for personal information.

**Prevention**
- Do not click unknown links in messages.
- Verify messages from banks or companies using official websites.
- Delete suspicious messages.

---

### 4. Baiting
Baiting tricks people by offering something attractive, such as free software or USB drives infected with malware.

**Prevention**
- Do not use unknown USB devices.
- Download software only from trusted sources.

---

### 5. Pretexting
Pretexting is when an attacker creates a fake identity or story to convince a victim to reveal confidential information.

**Prevention**
- Verify the identity of the person requesting information.
- Never share sensitive information without proper authorization.

---

## Best Practices to Prevent Social Engineering

- Use strong and unique passwords.
- Enable two-factor authentication.
- Stay alert for suspicious emails, calls, and messages.
- Verify requests before sharing sensitive information.
- Keep software and antivirus updated.
- Participate in security awareness training.

---

## Conclusion

Social engineering attacks target human behavior rather than computer systems. Awareness, careful verification, and good security practices help protect against these attacks and keep personal and organizational information safe.
